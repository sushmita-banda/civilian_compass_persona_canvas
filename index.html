About This Canvas
Built from one in-depth prospect interview with Marcus Cole, two founder calls with Langley (Civilian Compass founder), multiple documents Langley shared, and a clarity exercise run with the team. Ideal next step: additional interviews to confirm, challenge, or expand what Marcus showed us.

Section 03 — The Emotional Journey · Problem

The Emotional Arc

The emotional state Marcus is in when he encounters a message is as important as the message itself. Any message that meets him in Phase 1 when he's living in Phase 3 will miss entirely.

Phase 1 — Early Transition
Anxiety Masked as Overplanning
  • Starts tracking everything 16 months out
  • Wide net cast across every platform simultaneously
  • Overconfidence as a shield — "I've got this handled"
  • High energy, low filter — collecting resources, not acting on them
  • The structure of planning feels like control
Phase 2 — Active Search
Frustration at 2AM Rejections
  • Applications sent. Silence returned. Portals defeating qualified candidates.
  • First corporate veteran event: "thoughts and prayers"
  • "What am I without the uniform?" begins surfacing
  • Confidence erodes — not externally visible, internally real
  • The 10 years are starting to feel like they don't translate
Phase 3 — NOW
Doubt Creeping In
  • Narrowing from "best fit" to "most attainable"
  • Defense PM track starting to feel inevitable, not chosen
  • Family asks "so what are you doing?" — answer still unclear
  • The gap between who he is and what the job market sees is widening
  • The right conversation could shift all of this. Nobody has had it with him yet.

Section 05 — What Drives Him · Analysis

Motivations

Understanding what Marcus actually wants — beneath the job search tactics — is what separates positioning that earns trust from positioning that just sounds useful.

Identity
Lead, Not Just Execute
Marcus has led 100–200 people through high-stakes operations. He's not looking for a job — he's looking for the right arena to keep leading. The uniform was a context for that identity, not the source of it.
Security
Certainty for His Family
Income stopping on a known date with a family to support means every wasted week has a real number attached. He needs clarity fast, and he knows it. This urgency is not panic — it's precision.
Purpose
Prove the 10 Years Translated
Not to others — to himself. He wants the civilian world to recognize what a decade of command actually means, without him having to explain it to a recruiter who has never left the coast.
Fulfillment
A Path That Uses All of Him
Not just the clearance. Not just the PM skills. The intelligence background, the Environmental Science degree, the leadership instinct — he wants a role built around the whole person. He also knows what he doesn't want: a sales career is off the table, despite what the SkillBridge track suggests.
Legacy
Become the Older Guy
Unprompted, he described wanting to eventually be the mentor who puts his arm around the next Marcus. He's not just trying to get out of this — he's already thinking about what he'll give back when he does.
Agency
Integration, Not Isolation
"We're not a disabled class. We're just an isolated class." He wants to move through the civilian world on equal terms — chosen, not placed. Agency over charity, every time, in every message.
The Latent Aspiration — The Corridor Problem
Marcus is gravitating toward defense-adjacent PM roles — Northrop, L3Harris, ViaSat — because his clearance and UAS background make it the path of least resistance. TAPS conditioned him there. His peers default there.

But it's not where he wants to be in five years. He has an Environmental Science degree from Miami University. He named Corporate Sustainability as a secondary target, unprompted. Nobody has had the conversation with him that opens that aperture. He hasn't let himself ask the question because no one has told him it's available to ask.
"You don't have to be a project manager in defense the rest of your life." — Langley, Founder · Civilian Compass
Section 06 — In His Own Words · Analysis

What Hurts

These are Marcus's exact words from the interview. Not paraphrased. This is the language every Civilian Compass message needs to resonate with — because it's how Marcus talks when nobody is optimizing his words for a pitch deck.

🤖
The 2AM Rejection
"I got a rejection two days later. They didn't open it. It was over the weekend. What are we doing?"
Active clearance, UAS background, 10 years of command — rejected before any human saw it. The portal is the problem, not the candidate.

15 Platforms, Zero Integration
"We're inundated with stuff. I'm finding out new resources every day. No structured 'here's what you do in what order.'"
He's started cutting platforms because the overhead is unsustainable. The problem is never missing resources — it's always the missing sequence.
🙏
Thoughts & Prayers Events
"I went to a great one-day at Viasat. It seemed for show. 'Thanks for coming.' It's like thoughts and prayers."
Corporate veteran events feel performative. Hiring managers speak. Nothing follows. Every warm intro needs a next step attached — or it's theater.
🪪
The Identity Question
"We're not a disabled class. We're just an isolated class of people. I'm trying to poke some holes and get some integration with the populace."
The uniform was the identity. "Company commander" doesn't map to civilian ATS categories. He wants to be chosen, not charity. Integrated, not siloed.
🕳️
Where TAPS Stops
"TAPS was phenomenal — comprehensive resources. But then it ends, and there's no 'here's the next step.'"
TAPS gives a foundation and then drops him. The 9-month window between TAPS ending and SkillBridge starting belongs to nobody in the current ecosystem.
🚫
No Warm Handoffs Anywhere
"Some platforms say 'you'd be fantastic as a PM.' Cool. Where's the entry? Link me in with your buddy at X company."
Every resource hands Marcus to another resource. He doesn't need more information about opportunities. He needs someone to open a specific door to a specific person.

Positioning Implication for Civilian Compass
The market solves "get a veteran hired." Civilian Compass solves "help a veteran choose the right next chapter." These are different problems. Marcus represents exactly why that distinction matters — the system will get him hired in a role he didn't choose and won't want in five years. The conversation Civilian Compass needs to have with Marcus is not about his resume. It's about the Environmental Science degree he hasn't talked about in a job search context and the five-year vision he hasn't let himself articulate. That conversation doesn't exist anywhere else in the ecosystem right now.
Section 09 — How He Operates · Insight

Work Preferences

These aren't preferences in the abstract — they're behavioral patterns reinforced by 10 years of military operations. They tell you exactly how Civilian Compass must show up to earn Marcus's trust and keep it.

🎯
Structure Over Ambiguity
Marcus runs on orders he trusts. Give him a mission, a sequence, and clear success criteria — he will execute with precision. Open-ended "explore your options" frameworks do not work for someone who has operated against deadlines that don't move for 10 years.
Civilian Compass signal: the sequenced roadmap is not a feature. It is the product.
🤝
Peer Credibility Over Institutional Authority
He trusts veterans who made it — not programs, not recruiters, not LinkedIn influencers. The "older guy with his arm around him" is a veteran peer, not an institution. Credibility is earned through lived experience, not a logo or a credential.
Civilian Compass signal: Langley's voice, first person, peer-to-peer — always.
💬
Depth Over Broadcast
One real conversation outperforms ten events, ten newsletters, and ten LinkedIn posts combined. He went to a Viasat one-day and called it "thoughts and prayers." What he needs is a named human and a specific follow-through — not a room full of good intentions.
Civilian Compass signal: every warm intro comes with a next step attached.
🚪
Named Relationships Over Portals
"I don't want to send another resume into the ether. I want to get in front of a person." He does not need more platforms. He needs someone to say "call Dave at Viasat, he is expecting you." That sentence is worth more than all 15 platforms combined.
Civilian Compass signal: the warm handoff is not a feature — it is the whole point.
Section 10 — Lines That Cannot Be Crossed · Implication

Non-Negotiables

These are not preferences. These are the things that, if Civilian Compass gets them wrong, Marcus disengages permanently. Any message, any feature, any event that crosses one of these lines loses him before the second sentence.

Framework, not charity.
"We're not a disabled class. We're just an isolated class of people."
Do not frame Marcus as someone who needs rescuing. Frame him as someone who needs a framework. The difference between charity and agency is everything — and he will recognize the framing instantly.
Don't pick his path.
"Some platforms say 'you'd be fantastic as a PM.' Cool. Where's the entry?"
Do not narrow the aperture before the conversation begins. Do not hand him a job category — hand him a door. The corridor problem exists because every resource funnels him toward the same narrow path. Civilian Compass breaks that pattern or it is just another platform.
A person, not a portal.
"I don't want to send another resume into the ether. I want to get in front of a person."
Do not give Marcus another list of resources. Every resource he has leads to another resource. Civilian Compass is the one that breaks the loop — a specific person, a specific company, a real next step. That is the product. If Civilian Compass cannot deliver that, it belongs in the 15 he is already cutting.
No more thoughts and prayers.
"I went to a great one-day at Viasat. It seemed for show. 'Thanks for coming.' It's like thoughts and prayers."
Do not build another room of good intentions. Marcus has attended the events, heard the speeches, and shaken the hands. Every Civilian Compass touchpoint must end with something concrete — a name, a meeting, a defined next step. Good intentions with no follow-through is just another platform he will eventually cut.
